Falcon Hill: a state tax zone inside Roy.

Northrop Grumman's Roy Innovation Center sits in MIDA's Falcon Hill Project Area, where an appointed state board collects the property tax from new buildings. MIDA's own resolution names Roy City as a government that levies property tax inside the area. Here is what MIDA's records show about the money, next to Roy's own tax votes and its separate 1900 Southeast tax-increment area.

The short version

  • MIDA's Falcon Hill Project Area is about 550 acres of federal ground at Hill Air Force Base, leased for private development. MIDA's Resolution 2025-18 says Roy City levies a property tax inside it, along with Davis County, Weber County, Clearfield, Riverdale and Sunset.
  • In MIDA's June 2024 projection for FY2025, Falcon Hill buildings would produce about $4.6 million in property tax. MIDA projected sharing $801,320 of it (17.4%) with local taxing entities and keeping the rest for infrastructure, incentives, debt, municipal services and its own administration.
  • In November 2025, MIDA voted to give $50,000 each to the six local governments, Roy included, from leftover municipal services money. MIDA's Falcon Hill page puts FY2025 revenue at about $8.9 million.
  • MIDA's 2023 report to legislators also listed $49.3 million in state appropriations for Falcon Hill-area projects, including $31.7 million for the North Gate (5600 South) interchange.
  • Separately, Roy's own redevelopment agency has a 67.5-acre "1900 Southeast" area where six taxing entities agreed to send 70% of new property tax for 20 years, an estimated $24.9 million. Collection must start by Dec. 31, 2026.
  • Roy's 27.96% tax increase in 2025 was denied by the State Tax Commission on a notice rule. In August 2026 the council approved about 34.3%. Roy said both were for employee pay. No record found ties Roy's tax votes to Falcon Hill. The two are shown side by side here so readers can see both.

Start here: what Falcon Hill is

The Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) is a state authority the Legislature created in 2007. Its board is appointed, not elected.

MIDA's first project area was Falcon Hill, adopted in December 2008. It covers federal land at Hill Air Force Base that the Air Force leases out under an "enhanced use lease" (EUL). In 2009, MIDA and the Air Force partnered with Sunset Ridge Development Partners, a joint venture of Woodbury Corporation and Hunt Companies, to develop it.

Private buildings on the leased land pay property tax. Inside a MIDA project area, MIDA collects that tax and decides how it is used: on roads, utilities and buildings in the area, on incentives, on debt, on city-type services, on its own administration, and on a share passed back to local taxing entities. MIDA can also use part of the area's revenue as "municipal services revenue" and must split any leftover equally among the cities and counties that levy property tax there (Utah Code 63H-1-503).

The best-known tenant is Northrop Grumman's Roy Innovation Center, the headquarters campus for the Air Force's Sentinel intercontinental ballistic missile program. Ground was broken in August 2019. A sixth building, the "Legacy Building," broke ground July 14, 2026, with completion set for 2028.

The words used here, like "project area," "EUL" and "tax increment," are explained in Words to know.

Is Falcon Hill inside Roy?

Yes. MIDA said so itself. In Resolution 2025-18, adopted Nov. 17, 2025 and signed by MIDA Chair J. Stuart Adams, MIDA's board wrote that Davis County, Weber County, Clearfield City, Riverdale City, Roy City and Sunset City "each ... levy a property tax within the Falcon Hill Project Area." Under state law, that is what makes a city eligible for a share of leftover municipal services money, and Roy was paid on that basis.

Other records agree. A 2019 news report placed the Roy Innovation Center just south of the Hill Aerospace Museum, along Hill's border with Roy. Northrop Grumman datelines its own releases "ROY, Utah." MIDA's Sept. 18, 2026 board slides list a "Roy Innovation Expansion" in planning.

A wording note for anyone citing older pages: MIDA's web page has described the area as "touching" Roy and, more recently, as "adjacent to" Roy. Resolution 2025-18 is the more exact record, because it states that Roy levies property tax inside the area.

Roy's own side: two tax votes

Roy City sets its own property tax rate. Its last two requests went like this:

  • 2025: Roy proposed raising its property tax revenue 27.96%, or $1,423,978 a year. On a $432,000 home, the city portion would have gone from $384.44 to $492.54, up $108.10 a year. The council approved it after an Aug. 19, 2025 hearing. Residents filed a referendum petition on Aug. 25. In September, the State Tax Commission denied it, along with 34 other entities' increases, because Roy had not posted details of other proposed Weber County tax increases, a new requirement under S.B. 202 (2025) and S.B. 29 (2024). City Manager Matt Andrews said the Tax Commission's checklist did not list that requirement.
  • 2026: Roy proposed 55.45%, or $2,807,745. After a nearly four-hour hearing on Aug. 11, 2026, the council approved a smaller increase of about 34.3% on a 4-1 vote. Council members Diane Wilson, Jason Sphar, Alexis Jackson and Janel Hulbert voted yes; Bryon Saxton voted no. The city's packet lists $1,751,100 at 34.29%; the Standard-Examiner reported $1,749,050 after a vote removing council cost-of-living raises.

Roy's stated reason both years was employee pay and keeping staff. In its 2025 ballot argument, the city said 80 full-time employees had left in three years and that it had cut operating expenses 7.5% in the 2025 budget. Opponents argued for smaller increases or cuts.

Documented Roy levies property tax inside Falcon Hill (Resolution 2025-18). Roy received $50,000 from MIDA for FY2025-26. Roy raised taxes in 2026 after its 2025 increase was denied.
Not established How much of Falcon Hill's property tax comes from parcels inside Roy, what share Roy would have collected without the project area, and whether that would have changed Roy's budget. No record found connects Roy's tax votes to Falcon Hill, and Roy has not said they are connected.

Roy's own tax-increment area: 1900 Southeast

Separate from MIDA, Roy's redevelopment agency created the Amended 1900 Southeast Community Reinvestment Project Area, 67.52 acres in Roy bounded roughly by Riverdale Road, Interstate 15, 1900 West and 5600 South. The 2023 interlocal agreement with Weber County describes it as "vacant and underutilized land" planned for mixed-use residential and commercial development. When the agreement was drafted, the agency had no developer agreement yet.

  • What it produces now: $484,227 a year in property tax for all taxing entities. At the end of the period the agreement projects about $1,617,984 a year.
  • Deadline: the agency must trigger collection by Dec. 31, 2026, with the first payment in 2027. If it does not, Weber County may extend the deadline or end its agreement.
  • Who prepared the plan: Urban & Main Consulting. Weber County's signature block names Gage Froerer as Commission Chair.
  • Public notice: the agency published notice of the agreements, opening a 30-day window for anyone to contest them, as Utah law requires.
This area is Roy's own, not MIDA's. It sits near Falcon Hill's north gate at 5600 South, but no record ties the two together.

Numbers that don't match

These differences are between MIDA's and Northrop Grumman's own documents. None is shown as wrong; they may measure different things or come from different dates.

Acreage: 525 or 550?

MIDA's earlier page and earlier Hive pages: about 525 acres. MIDA's current page and Sept. 18, 2026 slides: about 550 acres.

Square feet leased: 1.3 million or 1.5 million?

MIDA's Sept. 18, 2026 board slides: 1.3 million square feet complete, 100% leased. MIDA's Oct. 21, 2026 slides to legislators and its Q1 2026 report: 1.5 million square feet fully leased.

Roy Innovation Center: how many people?

MIDA's page: capacity for more than 6,000 employees. Northrop Grumman's July 2026 release: capacity for more than 5,000. Standard-Examiner: upwards of 5,200. MIDA's Q1 2026 report counts about 6,000 as "new job growth" for all of Falcon Hill.

Building 1573: 750 or 800 people?

MIDA's Sept. 18, 2026 slides: an estimated 750-plus seats moved from the old 1200-series buildings. MIDA's Q1 2026 report: up to 800 personnel.

Falcon Hill's money: $4.6M or $8.9M?

The $4.6M is MIDA's projected property tax allocation for FY2025. The $8.9M on MIDA's web page is described as all FY2025 revenue for the project area. They are different measures.

Weighing it: transparency, money and the public

  • What was done in the open: MIDA's resolutions, budgets and reports to legislators are posted on the Utah Public Notice Website and le.utah.gov. Roy held hearings both years and posted its notices; its 2025 increase failed on one missing posting, not for lack of a hearing. Roy's agency published notice of its 1900 Southeast agreements.
  • What is hard to find: No posted record found breaks Falcon Hill's property tax down by city, so Roy residents can't see how much comes from inside Roy. MIDA reports the money in several different ways across documents.
  • Money back to the public: MIDA projected sharing 17.4% of Falcon Hill's FY2025 property tax with local taxing entities, plus the $300,000 split six ways. It also paid off and released the 2017 assessment bonds and lists $3.5 million in environmental cleanup.
  • Who decides: MIDA's board and Falcon Hill's DRC are appointed. Roy's council is elected and answered to residents at two hearings and a referendum petition.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask

  • MIDA board: How much of Falcon Hill's property tax comes from parcels inside Roy, and will you post that breakdown each year?
