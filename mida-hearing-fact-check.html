MIDA told legislators its side. Here's what the records say.

On Oct. 21, 2026, the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA), a state authority run by an appointed board, answered a legislative committee's questions for about an hour. The Hive pulled 32 statements from the official hearing video. Each one below is checked against state law, MIDA's own budgets and presentations, and the agreements MIDA signed with counties.

The short version

  • Most of what MIDA said lines up with the records. 13 of the 32 statements mostly match, including county consent, the Box Elder vote and the Air Force request behind Stratos.
  • MIDA said it gets "zero state money." MIDA's own 2023 presentation to the Legislature lists $71,194,600 in "Legislative Appropriations" for projects in fiscal years 2022 through 2024. The table doesn't show this year, or how much was paid to MIDA itself.
  • MIDA said the State Auditor is auditing it "right now." The Auditor's own MIDA page says the office "does not audit MIDA." A news report says the office asked for documents. The Hive found no published announcement of an audit.
  • MIDA said counties keep control of land use. Box Elder's agreement says the MIDA board appoints the review committee (the county recommends 7 of 8), and Wasatch County's says "no submissions to or approvals by the County shall be required."
  • A promise that money goes back to every taxing entity if a project finishes early was not found in state law or the agreements checked.

How this file was checked: The 32 statements were pulled from the committee's official video of the Oct. 21, 2026 hearing, using its auto-generated captions, with the video time for each. Quotes come from those captions; the audio has not yet been checked word for word, and garbled words are not quoted. Each statement was then checked against the records linked in Sources. Every document was read through PDF text extraction or a web page pull, not rendered pages, so a few items are marked for a second look. Anything not yet confirmed is labeled on its card.

Start here: what MIDA is

The Legislature created the Military Installation Development Authority in 2007 (S.B. 232, 2007). It is run by a board that is appointed, not elected.

MIDA develops land on or near military sites. In its project areas, MIDA keeps up to 75% of the new property tax that comes from growth, for 25 years per parcel, and its board can add 15 more years. State law says project area land is not subject to county land-use law (Utah Code 63H-1-202(2)). MIDA's website lists 8 project areas. Three of them came up most at the hearing: Falcon Hill at Hill Air Force Base, the Mayflower area in Wasatch County, and Stratos in Box Elder County.

The words used on this page, like "tax increment," "interlocal agreement" and "review committee," are explained in Words to know.

Money
#14 · "We have no money... Everything is tax increment." "We get zero state money."
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:48:02 and 1:59:01 (captions)
Not supported by the records found

MIDA's own presentation lists state money. MIDA's Sept. 18, 2023 presentation to the Legislature has a table titled "Legislative Appropriations." It lists $71,194,600 for fiscal years 2021-22 through 2023-24 (the table is below). A MIDA overview given to lawmakers around 2014 reported $20.89 million appropriated since 2008. State law also provides for state appropriations and loans to MIDA (63H-1-104 and 63H-1-706).

Context, in fairness to MIDA: in the captions, the "zero state money" line comes during talk about private money at Mayflower and the military rooms, and is followed by "All of our money, our admin, everything comes from local tax increment." The 2023 table lists money appropriated for projects. It does not show whether the money was paid to MIDA itself, whether it was for operations or construction, or anything about this year.

Not found yet: whether MIDA received state money in FY2026 or FY2027. The FY2027 budget form's columns came out jumbled in a text pull and need a look at the rendered page.

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). The Hive's Sept. 4, 2026 notes read MIDA's FY2026 budget filing ("Fiscal Year Ended 6/30/2026," utah.gov/pmn/files/1453631.pdf) as a text-based PDF. It lists "Intergovernmental revenue" of $26,391,863 (FY2025 actual), $31,317,916 (current year) and $31,150,387 (proposed). The filing does not say which governments that money comes from, so it does not settle whether any of it is state money. (Notion's notes say this filing could not be opened on Oct. 24; the Sept. 4 read is kept here with its method.)

Sources: MIDA presentation to the Legislature (Sept. 18, 2023), "Legislative Appropriations" table; MIDA overview to the Legislature (about 2014); Utah Code 63H-1-104 and 706; MIDA FY2027 budget form.

#12 · If a project finishes early, "all the revenues then go back to all the taxing authorities" (the "waterfall").
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:24:32 (captions)
Not supported by the records found

Not found in state law or the Box Elder agreement. No clause returning money to every taxing entity on early completion turned up in either.

What is there: Wasatch County's 2023 West Side agreement sends leftover Development Fund money to the county after MIDA's list of projects. That money goes to the county only, and it is not tied to early completion. State law lets MIDA use the money "before, during, and after" the collection period.

Not checked yet: the Falcon Hill and Wasatch East Side agreements. "Not found in the records checked" is narrower than saying the clause doesn't exist anywhere.

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-501(1)(a)(ii); Box Elder Agreement 26-15; MIDA Res. 2023-01 with the Wasatch West Side agreement.

#11 · Project areas run 25 years, plus 15 (up to 40) only with the local land use authority. "No MIDA project area is forever."
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:24:00 (captions)
Partly matches the record

25 plus 15 matches the law (63H-1-501).

Doesn't match: the extra 15 years is the MIDA board's call ("if the board determines..."). The statute doesn't require local approval for it. The clock also runs parcel by parcel, and money may be used "before, during, and after" the collection period. Box Elder's agreement runs 50 years, and its end doesn't change the project area's boundaries.

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-501; Box Elder Agreement 26-15; MIDA Res. 2023-01 (Wasatch West Side agreement).

#10 · The tax split is negotiated in an interlocal agreement, "not a take it or leave it."
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:24:00 (captions)
Matches the record (mostly)

Box Elder's agreement is a negotiated package: energy taxes, a 3.5% telecom tax, a 1% room tax, a sales tax share, 25% of dedicated tax collections (not counting any Compute Complex), and a $10,000-per-megawatt minimum.

Context: the law caps MIDA at "up to 75%," and a county's consent can't be taken back. In 2019, Wasatch County's manager said the state "somewhat forced our hand" (KPCW, May 31, 2019).

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). The draft Stratos Master Development Agreement (§13), which MIDA's own document page lists as approved "in substantial form" but still "a draft and unsigned out of respect for the current referendum process," splits the remaining Compute Complex payments 80% to the Master Developer and 20% to MIDA, with part of MIDA's 20% going to the State. Box Elder County is not part of that split; the county's share comes through the interlocal agreement terms above. (Hive notes, Sept. 4, 2026, read from the posted documents.)

Sources: Box Elder Agreement 26-15 §6; Box Elder Stratos timeline; MIDA Res. 2023-01; KPCW (May 31, 2019).

#13 · Money can't leave a project area, except for Stratos (the Guard and Hill AFB) and outside infrastructure that benefits the area.
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:25:02 (captions)
Matches the record (mostly)

Matches: 63H-1-502(1)(e) allows infrastructure outside an area if the board finds it benefits the area, and the board's decision "is final."

Left out: 502(1)(g) also allows spending on military morale, welfare and recreation programs. MIDA has also made partner payments outside its areas: $50,000 each to six Falcon Hill neighbors (Res. 2025-18), $300,000 to the Hill Aerospace Museum and $100,000 to the Wasatch School District.

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). S.B. 316 (2025), primary sponsor Sen. Jerry Stevenson, MIDA's vice chair, is described in the Hive's notes as also letting MIDA move project revenue outside a project area's boundary. See #21 and the Jerry Stevenson profile.

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-502; utah.gov/pmn/files/1352399.pdf; MIDA Falcon Hill page; utah.gov/pmn/files/1164231.pdf.

Oversight and openness
#8 · MIDA has a required annual audit; the State Auditor is auditing MIDA "right now"; the Legislature has audited MIDA.
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 2:03:31 to 2:04:00 (captions)
Partly matches the record

Annual audit: matches. State law requires one (63H-1-704), and MIDA posted its 2026 audited financials in October. That audit is done by a private firm.

State Auditor "right now": the State Auditor's own MIDA page says the office "does not audit MIDA." The Auditor's May 28, 2026 release calls its new MIDA website "an informational tool." FOX 13 reported after the hearing that Hemmert said the Auditor's office had requested documents. The Hive found no published announcement of a State Auditor audit. A web page alone can't rule out a newer review, so this stays "partly."

Legislative audit: no earlier legislative audit of MIDA was found in the sources checked.

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-704; State Auditor MIDA page; State Auditor release (May 28, 2026); FOX 13 (Oct. 21, 2026).

#6 · Meetings are posted on MIDA's website and the state Public Notice Website; there's no mass email.
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:22:02 (captions)
Matches the record (mostly)

Matches: the state Public Notice Website has MIDA's notices (for example April 24 and June 26, 2026). MIDA's website has an email signup for news.

Also at the hearing: Rep. James Dunnigan said posting on MIDA's website is "not adequate notice to the public." Hemmert answered that a local notice requirement "might be a great modification." State law says MIDA is "not required to physically post notice" (63H-1-202(7)). The Hive found no law requiring newspaper or mailed notice of MIDA board meetings; narrower notice rules are in 63H-1-402(2) and 403(1).

Sources: Public Notice 1075195; Public Notice 1091257; MIDA transparency page; Utah Code 63H-1-202(7), 402, 403.

#7 · MIDA is subject to the Open and Public Meetings Act; all board meetings are public, recorded and posted.
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:29:31 (captions)
True, with context left out

True. The April 24 and June 26, 2026 notices have audio and minutes attached. The April 24 meeting, where MIDA's board adopted the Stratos plan, was noticed April 22 at 7:29 p.m. That meets the Act's 24 hours, about a day and a half ahead.

Left out: the law applies the Act to MIDA with exceptions, including no physical posting and no anchor location for electronic meetings (63H-1-202(7)(a)).

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-202(7); Public Notices 1075195 and 1091257.

MIDA is subject to GRAMA, Utah's public records law (63H-1-202(8)). No public count of its requests was found.

Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:30:04 (captions)

The history notes in the MIDA Act show changes in 2007, 2009, 2010, 2013, 2015, every year from 2019 through 2026, and the 2025 First Special Session. The gap years may have had bills that don't show in today's code.

#20 · MIDA is an independent entity because a state agency would run into the state constitution's limits (the "ripper clause").
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:28:01 (captions)
Matches the record (mostly)

63H-1-201(2) makes MIDA an independent body corporate and politic and a political subdivision. Utah Constitution Article VI, Section 28 bars handing city functions to special commissions. The "why" is a legal reading, not something a record can prove.

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). A court case is testing this. Alliance for a Better Utah has sued in Utah's 3rd District Court, naming MIDA, Box Elder County, and Sen. Stuart Adams and Sen. Jerry Stevenson. Utah News Dispatch quoted the complaint saying the constitution "forbids plural office holding by legislators" (Article VI, Section 6), and the complaint says MIDA's structure puts lawmaking power over "taxation, zoning, land use" in "a non-elected MIDA Board and the Interlocal Agreement's special commission." The Hive has not yet confirmed whether the complaint cites Article VI, Section 28 by number, and has found no final ruling as of its notes. Read from news coverage and Better Utah's posted materials, not the full complaint PDF.

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-201; Utah Constitution Art. VI Sec. 28.

Local control and consent
#3 · The local land use authority keeps control through the interlocal agreement and a review committee; Box Elder's has about 9 members, 8 appointed by the county.
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:31:00 (captions)
Partly matches the record

There is a review committee. But Agreement 26-15 §3(b) says it has 8 members, chaired by the MIDA director. The county recommends the other 7, the MIDA board appoints them, and they "serve at the pleasure of the MIDA Board."

Also in the record: §3(a) gives MIDA the "exclusive right" to police powers over the private land, and state law exempts project area land from county land-use law (63H-1-202(2)). See Who controls land use.

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). More of the exchange, from the Hive's own voice-typed notes (not yet checked against the official video): Hemmert said the county "still controls that Design Review Committee" and decisions are made "in most cases, almost all cases, with approval from the county explicitly." Asked by Rep. Dunnigan who sits on it, he said the county appoints the members and "it's usually staff members," such as planning and fire department staff, and agreed the committee has final approval. Dunnigan answered: "In a city, it's going to go back to the legislative body for final approval, and the elected officials can be held accountable." Hemmert also said a rezone from agricultural to industrial goes to the committee. Rep. Larson read 63H-1-202 aloud at the hearing.

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). In fairness to the county: Box Elder County's May 4, 2026 release lists a seat for one local landowner on the Development Review Committee among the terms the county negotiated (Hive notes, Sept. 4, 2026).

Added Nov. 2, 2026 (second pass). Moratoriums: the draft, unsigned Master Development Agreement (§3(f)) says the project area "shall be excluded from any moratorium" unless MIDA's board finds one necessary. Box Elder later adopted a 180-day data center moratorium (Ordinance 654). Because the agreement is still a draft, how this applies is not settled.

Sources: Box Elder Agreement 26-15 §3; Utah Code 63H-1-202(2); Governor's Office Stratos FAQ.

#4 · In Wasatch, outside the military hotel piece, permits and approvals "100% goes to the county."
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:35:04 (captions)
Partly matches the record

The county does the permit work. The 2023 West Side agreement has the county perform permitting and inspection services (§7(c)).

But the same agreement says "MIDA shall retain and exercise full and exclusive jurisdiction, including all land use authority," and "no submissions to or approvals by the County shall be required" (§8(b)). Wasatch County's own page says MIDA keeps full land use authority west of Highway 40.

Sources: MIDA Res. 2023-01 (Wasatch West Side agreement); Wasatch County Jordanelle page; KPCW (May 31, 2019).

#5 · "We kind of just act as the RDA for the county" (Wasatch).
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:49:31 (captions)
Partly matches the record

The money flow looks like an RDA's: after MIDA's priority list, the county decides how remaining Development Fund money is used (§10(d)(iii)).

The difference: unlike a city or county redevelopment agency, MIDA holds the land use authority on the west side (see #4). This is his description, not a provable fact.

Sources: MIDA Res. 2023-01; Park Record (Oct. 31, 2023).

#2 · MIDA "cannot create a project area without the consent of every landowner... and the land use authority."
Dan Hemmert · official video, about 1:20:03 (captions)
Matches the record (mostly)

63H-1-401(3) requires the owners' consent and the consent of the county or city legislative body (the law's term, not "land use authority"). The rule covers land that isn't military land. Box Elder Agreement 26-15 §2 matches.

Sources: Utah Code 63H-1-401(3); Box Elder Agreement 26-15.

#28 · The project replaces a 7-room military facility at Snowbasin, torn down for the 2002 Olympics, that the state promised to replace; Park City didn't want it.
Dan Hemmert, with Sen. Ann Millner · official video, about 1:46:30 (captions)

Matches: it was torn down for the Olympics, and Park City and Summit County opposed the replacement there.

Doesn't match: KPCW in 2023 called it a "44-room ski chalet" (the captions may have misheard the number). The replacement site came from 2001 federal legislation (Rep. Jim Hansen) that gave the Air Force a Park City parcel. That was a congressional arrangement, not a state promise.

Sources: utah.gov/pmn/files/1164231.pdf; KPCW (Oct. 20, 2023); Hill AFB article on the MWR hotel; Park Record (2021).

What this hearing teaches

  • Check what an agency told lawmakers before. The "zero state money" table came from MIDA's own 2023 presentation to the same Legislature.
  • Ask which audit. A yearly financial audit by a private firm and a State Auditor audit are two different things.
  • Read who appoints. "The county recommends" and "the MIDA board appoints" are both true of the same committee.
  • Give credit where the record backs it. County consent, the unanimous Box Elder vote, posted meetings and the Air Force request all check out.

Questions to ask, and whom to ask
