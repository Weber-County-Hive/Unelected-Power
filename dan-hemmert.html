Four stages, one office

Dan Hemmert's career traces a path that has since become familiar at the top of Utah's economic development office: elected official, then agency director, then private-sector operator with business before the same state, then director of another state authority. He resigned as a state senator — he had served as Senate Majority Whip, representing District 13/24 — in January 2021 to become Gov. Spencer Cox's first executive director of the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity (GOEO), a role he held for roughly a year before returning to the private sector.

From 2023 to early 2026, Hemmert built a private lobbying practice with a client list weighted heavily toward developers and companies with direct interests before the state agencies he used to help run — Dakota Pacific Real Estate, Alterra Mountain Company, the Utah Association of REALTORS, Lincoln Property Company, Union Pacific Railroad, Zions Bancorporation, Sanofi US, and, notably, the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) itself. On Jan. 13, 2026, he terminated most of that client roster — keeping only clients with direct MIDA jurisdiction. He is now MIDA's Executive Director.

Before GOEO: the Senate record

In 2019, as a state senator, Hemmert co-sponsored — with Rep. Marc Roberts — the legislation creating Utah's "Regulatory Sandbox," signed by Gov. Gary Herbert. The law lets participating companies test innovative financial products and services in the Utah market for a limited period without first obtaining the state licenses or authorizations that would otherwise be required, administered through the Department of Commerce. It's a piece of his legislative record worth having on file alongside the web of financial-vehicle LLCs (including LH Capital, discussed below) he has personally built since leaving office.

Months before Gov. Cox appointed him to lead the state's economic development office, Hemmert had already taken a public role in weighing business interests against public health guidance during a state emergency. As a state senator in April 2020, Hemmert co-sponsored the bill creating the Public Health and Economic Emergency Commission — a 10-member body formed after Senate President Stuart Adams pushed to limit local governments' authority to set their own COVID-19 restrictions. His co-sponsor was Rep. Mike Schultz, later Utah's House Speaker. Hemmert took a seat on the commission he helped create.

Of the ten members, only two had medical or public health backgrounds. The rest included Adams, Hemmert, Schultz, the president and CEO of the Salt Lake Chamber of Commerce, and the CEO of the Larry H. Miller Group of Companies. According to ProPublica's reporting (July 21, 2020), Utah's state epidemiologist and the state health department's executive director were not commission members and were not present for its key votes.

On May 29, 2020, Hemmert was one of six commission members present who voted unanimously to move most of the state to its least-restrictive "green" reopening level, effective June 5 — a decision the state epidemiologist told reporters the following day no community in Utah was actually ready for. Case counts, which had plateaued in the weeks before the vote, began surging shortly after.

Two of Hemmert's fellow commission members are separately documented elsewhere in this project's reporting: Rep. Mike Schultz, his co-sponsor on the bill creating the commission, and Senate President Stuart Adams, who pushed for the commission's creation and moved the vote to accelerate reopening further.

A personal business tie to the sitting Attorney General

Utah Division of Corporations records (Entity #12262272-0160, formed April 20, 2021, active, renewed March 30, 2026, next renewal due April 30, 2027) list Daniel Hemmert as LH Capital, LLC's registered agent and its only listed principal — Manager, at 1115 E 965 N, Orem. OpenCorporates' own record of the entity's officer history shows the same pattern going back further: Hemmert has held every officer position the entity has ever had — agent, registered agent, and now manager — with no other person ever listed, active or inactive. LH Capital is classified under NAICS code 52599 ("Other Financial Vehicles") and UN ISIC code 6430 ("Trusts, funds and similar financial entities"), consistent with a personal investment vehicle rather than an operating business. Attorney General Derek Brown's own official financial disclosure form separately lists LH Capital, LLC as an entity where Brown holds a Member position. Utah's LLC filing requirements don't require every member to be named on the state record, so Brown's absence from LH Capital's own filing isn't a contradiction — but the membership tie is currently sourced entirely to Brown's own disclosure, not corroborated by LH Capital's corporate registration itself.

Hemmert was previously a named partner at Election Hive LLC, a campaign infrastructure firm whose registered address — 136 S Main St, Suite A200, Salt Lake City — matches the address used by Derek Brown's own Attorney General campaign committee. Hemmert's profile was removed from Election Hive's site around the time of his MIDA appointment. Election Hive has separately been documented in this project's reporting as paying more than $114,000 to Brown's campaign and more than $186,000 to Gov. Cox in 2025, and its client list includes a wide swath of sitting Utah legislators.

Not yet verified: what LH Capital's financial-vehicle classification actually holds or invests in, whether it has any state-related business, and when Brown became a member relative to Hemmert's MIDA appointment. Also worth noting: the membership tie rests on Brown's own disclosure alone — LH Capital's state filing and its full OpenCorporates officer history name no member besides Hemmert.

What this would trigger in corporate America

None of what follows is a claim that Hemmert broke a specific law — he has not been charged with anything. It's a comparison of governance mechanisms: what these fact patterns would typically set in motion at a publicly traded company, versus what actually happened here.

What happenedWhat it would typically trigger in corporate America
Lobbied for an authority, then terminated most other clients and became that same authority's Executive DirectorA cooling-off period and formal recusal review before an outside advisor could be hired into a role overseeing former clients — most public companies bar exactly this kind of direct advisor-to-executive transition without an independent review
An LLC he controls (HEMMCO) making a PAC donation six days before that PAC donated to the sitting Attorney General's campaign, while Brown's own disclosure names Hemmert's LH Capital as an entity where Brown holds a Member positionMandatory disclosure of the business relationship as a related-party matter, reviewed independently of the individuals involved
A company he controls received $62,500 from a taxpayer-funded nonprofit, a payment that went missing from that nonprofit's own year-end report to its state funderAn auditor finding on the funder's books, and a compliance review of the vendor relationship
The same accountability gap documented on Ryan Starks' page applies here — no cooling-off requirement, no independent review board, no mandatory disclosure regime governs the movement of a single person through agency leadership, private lobbying of that same agency's counterparts, and back into public authority leadership.

Open questions

What is Hemcik Holdings Costa Mesa LLC, and is it actually connected to Hemmert?

Filed June 4, 2026, organizer/registered agent William J. Sefcik (Mapleton, UT address). No proof of ownership or involvement by Hemmert has been found — this is flagged as unverified naming-pattern speculation only, not to be treated as a real connection without an Operating Agreement, bank records, or correspondence establishing the tie.

Did Hemmco represent both Meta and the City of Eagle Mountain on the same incentive fight?

A secondary, non-primary source (a municipal-intelligence data product) claims Hemmco was hired directly by Eagle Mountain to lobby against a state bill threatening the city's data center tax incentives, while Hemmco separately represents Meta — the incentive beneficiary — on the same facility. This specific bill has not been independently verified via direct search. If true, this would mean Hemmert represented both sides of the same incentive fight simultaneously.

When did Brown become a member of LH Capital relative to Hemmert's MIDA appointment?

Not yet established from any source found. The sequencing matters for understanding whether the business relationship predates or follows Hemmert's move into public authority leadership.

Does MIDA's own conflict-of-interest process address Hemmert's former client list?

Not yet reviewed. MIDA board minutes and any recusal record covering matters involving Hemmert's former clients (particularly Dakota Pacific) would be the place this should appear, if it exists.

Is "ALL IN FOR UTAH" (2023 nonprofit) the same organization as "All In For Utah PAC" (formed 2026)?

Hemmert is listed as a director of a nonprofit called ALL IN FOR UTAH, registered January 27, 2023. A separate filing elsewhere in this research shows Hemmert as Treasurer of "All In For Utah PAC," formed April 17, 2026, with Owen Fuller as Chair. Whether these are the same organization re-filed under a different structure, or two distinct entities that happen to share a name, has not been established.

What is the UAC Housing Foundation, and does it connect to the UWHA housing-advocacy research?

Hemmert was director and incorporator of UAC Housing Foundation from August 2024 until it went inactive in November 2025. Not yet researched — worth checking against the already-documented Utah Workforce Housing Advocacy (UWHA) funding and personnel for any overlap.

Are the Idaho entities (Liberty pipeline inc, Liberty Town Canal Company) the same Dan Hemmert?

Likely yes. Both are registered at 4795 Lanark Rd, Ovid, Idaho — a different address from the Utah entities — and Liberty pipeline inc names its officer as "Dan W Hemmert"/"Dan William Hemmert," a middle name absent from the Utah filings. A Whitepages record for "Daniel W Hemmert," however, lists his address as 1115 E 965 N, Orem — matching every confirmed Utah entity — and his work as "Partner at Hemmco, LLC," his own firm. That address-and-employer match is strong corroborating evidence this is the same person, though Whitepages is a data aggregator rather than a primary government record. A minor supporting detail: a 2010 USU hydrology thesis on the Bear River Basin lists a water right for "Hemmert Hot Spring" (priority date 1898) in the Bear Lake National Wildlife Refuge area — the same stretch of Bear Lake County, Idaho as Ovid. A named landmark carrying the family name in that exact area, dating back over a century, is consistent with a long-standing family presence there, though this is a place-name detail from 1898, not proof of the current entities' ownership.

What are the Geothermal Impact Alliance and Liberty pipeline inc?

Both are recent director/officer roles — Geothermal Impact Alliance (Utah, since May 2025) and Liberty pipeline inc (an Idaho nonprofit, since March 2026). Neither has been researched yet. Both names suggest energy-sector activity, which would be worth cross-referencing against this project's existing energy-policy research if either turns out to be substantive.
