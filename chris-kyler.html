Two roles, one person

Chris Kyler is chief executive of the Utah Association of Realtors (UAR), the statewide trade group for Utah's real estate industry. He is also a partner at Kyler Kohler Ostermiller & Sorensen, LLP (KKOS), the government-affairs firm that carries his own name — and through it, he is UAR's own registered lobbyist. The organization he leads is also his own client.

His current lobbyist.utah.gov registration lists his business address as 230 W Towne Ridge Pkwy Ste 500, Sandy — the same suite as UAR's own offices. He has been registered as a Utah lobbyist continuously since December 2012.

What UAR pays him

Per UAR's 2024 Form 990 (filed with the IRS, available through ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer): $834,163 in reported compensation, plus $74,344 in other compensation — a combined total of $908,507.

Who else pays him

Kyler's current lobbyist registration lists 30 active principals — clients paying him to represent their interests to state government, alongside his day job running UAR. The client roster spans real estate and housing groups, but also reaches well outside that industry:

Every principal's stated purpose on the public filing uses nearly identical boilerplate language — "legislative advocacy and consulting; monitor relevant legislation and influence public policy" — giving little client-specific detail about what he's actually being paid to do for each one.

The Kyler-Ostermiller team

Kyler isn't lobbying alone. Fellow KKOS partner Mike Ostermiller — CEO of the Northern Wasatch Association of Realtors and a Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) board member since 2008 — registers and drops several of the same clients on nearly identical dates: Blue Castle Holdings (2014-2016), Sutherland Institute (2020-), Critical Infrastructure Materials Coalition (2019-), the Utah Inland Port Authority (2022), and Libertas Institute, among others. The register shows the two KKOS partners adding and dropping these same clients on matching dates, which goes beyond two lobbyists who happen to work at the same firm.

A Feb. 12, 2012 Deseret News column by veteran political columnists Frank Pignanelli and LaVarr Webb named the two directly: "Under the leadership of Chris Kyler and Michael Ostermiller, the Realtors often prevail." The description of their partnership and legislative reach is not new — it was already public, mainstream knowledge 14 years before this file was compiled.

Both men were also named as co-defendants, along with the Northern Wasatch Association of Realtors, in Bates v. Utah Association of Realtors MLS (Utah Court of Appeals, 2013). The case was dismissed on statute-of-limitations grounds and never reached the merits.

Open questions

What is Kyler's client listed only as "APS"?

A principal on his current registration is listed as "APS," address 12894 S Pony Express, Draper, UT, purpose stated as "education management services." That address and description are consistent with American Preparatory Schools/Academy, but no entity registered under the exact name "APS" turns up in a Division of Corporations search — lobbyist filings often use informal client shorthand rather than a registered legal name. Not confirmed as the same organization.

Does his UAR registered-agent title match UAR's own leadership listing?

Kyler is UAR's registered agent of record with the state, at UAR's own office address. UAR's corporate filing lists Steven Bond as President, with Stephanie Taylor as Vice President and Robert Ockey as Treasurer — while other sourcing describes Taylor and Ockey with different titles. Corporate officer-of-record titles and an association's own internal leadership titles can differ; this hasn't been reconciled against UAR's own current website or Form 990 listing.
