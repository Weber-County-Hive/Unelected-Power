What Utah Rising is

Utah Rising: A Free Enterprise Vision for Utah's Economy is a business-led, statewide economic strategy jointly presented by the Utah Chamber and Salt Lake Chamber. Utah Rising's own materials give two different launch dates: an August 5, 2026 press release says "fall 2024," while a January 2026 news post on Utah Rising's own site says "April 2024" — not yet reconciled. The initiative is reviewed and updated by Utah business leaders every two years. Its Steering Committee is chaired by Spencer P. Eccles; Derek Miller, President & CEO of the Utah Chamber, is quoted extensively on its materials but holds a separate role. Per its own materials, it sets goals across six focus areas — workforce and education, housing, transportation, livability, business environment, and natural resources — each paired with a named "signature project," including a $1 billion investment in mental health-professional training and doubling FrontRunner rail service along the Wasatch Front.

On April 29, 2025, then-GOEO Executive Director Ryan Starks and the state's Industrial Assistance Account Committee approved a 10-year, $3.2 million taxpayer grant to the Salt Lake Chamber Foundation for Utah Rising — three days before Starks announced he was leaving GOEO to lead EDCUtah, an organization under the same Chamber's umbrella. The grant was later canceled by Starks' successor over conflict-of-interest concerns. Full detail on that sequence is on Ryan Starks' own case file.

Utah Rising's own stated opportunities and challenges

Per its own "Opportunities & Challenges" page, Utah Rising frames its strategy around five identified economic opportunities and five identified challenges.

Five opportunities, per the initiative's own framing:

  • The "AI-age" — positioning Utah's workforce to compete in artificial intelligence
  • An "energy decade" — leveraging Utah's mixed energy portfolio, including oil, gas, coal, geothermal, wind, solar, biomass, hydroelectric, hydrogen, storage, critical minerals, and what the page calls "potential modern nuclear"
  • A new downtown sports, entertainment, culture, and convention district, citing over $3 billion in private investment and $1 billion in public investment, the arrival of the Utah Hockey Club, the possibility of an MLB team, and nearby investment by the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints near Temple Square
  • Four "generational real estate investments": The Point (600 acres of state land at the Salt Lake/Utah County line), the Inland Port, Utah City (an 800-acre Utah Lake shoreline project), and Falcon Hill (aerospace/defense expansion near Hill Air Force Base)
  • The 2034 Winter Olympics and Paralympics, with an estimated $6.6 billion economic impact

Five challenges, per the same page:

  • A mismatch between workforce skills and the needs of a knowledge economy
  • Housing affordability
  • Growth pressure on energy availability and the environment
  • Rising youth and young-adult mental health concerns
  • Utahns experiencing intergenerational poverty and homelessness
Two of the four "generational real estate investments" Utah Rising names are already extensively documented elsewhere in this project: the Inland Port (Utah Inland Port Authority, including Golden Spike) and The Point (which connects to the Colmena Group/Lance Bullen network on Dan Hemmert's own case file). Falcon Hill is confirmed elsewhere in this project as MIDA's only Weber County project. Utah City, an 800-acre Utah Lake development, has no dedicated research anywhere in this project yet.

Still active through the 2026 session

The grant's cancellation in July 2025 didn't end the initiative. Utah Rising's own website lists recent activity including a piece titled "Advocacy in Action: Utah Rising Priorities Take Center Stage in the 2026 Legislative Session," a legislative recap titled "From Vision to Action: 2026 Legislative Session Outcomes," and webinars on specific focus-area topics ("Water 101," among others). The initiative also maintains a video series with one entry per focus area — Workforce, Transportation, Business Environment, Housing, Livability, and Natural Resources.

The Chamber's own 2026 Legislative Scorecard gives the precise figure behind that claim: 17 priority bills, 100% success rate, out of 260 business-related bills the Chamber tracked during a session that introduced over 1,000 bills, passed more than 540, and appropriated a $31.63 billion budget. The Chamber names four priority areas its advocacy advanced that session: expanding housing supply, strengthening the workforce talent pipeline, accelerating critical mineral development, and long-term Great Salt Lake protection — the last two directly overlapping with existing research elsewhere in this project (S.B. 254's critical-minerals framework, and this project's extensive Great Salt Lake reporting). At a June 18, 2026 ceremony at the Utah Capitol, the Chamber named four "Free Enterprise Champion" legislators — Sen. Scott Sandall, Sen. Evan Vickers, Rep. Doug Owens, and Rep. Karen Peterson — plus Rocky Mountain Power President Dick Garlish, honored separately as a distinguished member. The specific list of which 17 bills counted as "priority" is published only inside an interactive flipbook document, not as readable text; it has not been reviewed for this piece.

In an August 5, 2026 press release announcing a new action-plan phase, the Utah Chamber stated that it "achieved a 100% success rate on its priority legislation" during the 2026 session, including policies the release says advanced Utah Rising's stated goals.

That means Utah Rising was an active legislative advocacy presence — one the Chamber itself says was fully successful — during the same 2026 session that produced both H.B. 545 (the Legislature's unfunded response to the audit's IAA findings) and OLAG Report 2026-18 itself.

Derek Miller has also remained a prominent public voice specifically on Utah Rising's Workforce and Education focus area — telling Deseret News that AI could be the state's largest economic shift since the 1869 golden spike, and appearing on a May 19, 2026 Axios Live panel on AI, sponsored by Anthropic, alongside GOED Executive Director Jefferson Moss and Rep. Jennifer Dailey-Provost.

The family connection

Utah Rising's Steering Committee includes The Larry H. Miller Company. Its CEO is Steve Starks — Ryan Starks' brother. The same grant Ryan Starks personally approved went to an initiative whose governing Steering Committee includes a company run by his own sibling, alongside GOEO, his own office at the time.

Open questions

Did Utah Rising launch in April 2024 or fall 2024?

Utah Rising's own materials give two different answers. An August 5, 2026 Utah Chamber press release says "fall 2024." A January 2026 Utah Rising news post says "April 2024." Not reconciled.

Which specific 17 bills count as the Utah Chamber's 2026 "priority legislation"?

The number (17) and the 100% success rate are confirmed directly from the Chamber's own 2026 Legislative Scorecard page. The actual list of bills is published only inside an interactive Issuu flipbook document embedded on that page, which isn't extractable as readable text with the tools used for this research.

What is Utah City, and who is behind it?

Named by Utah Rising itself as one of four "generational real estate investments" — an 800-acre project on the shores of Utah Lake. No dedicated research on this project exists anywhere else in this project yet.

Who is Spencer P. Eccles, and does he have other ties documented elsewhere in this project?

Confirmed as chair of Utah Rising's Steering Committee (per the Utah Chamber's own August 5, 2026 press release). Not yet researched against any other file in this project — worth checking given how common the Eccles name is in Utah banking and philanthropy.

Did anyone flag GOEO's own committee membership as a conflict before the $3.2M grant was approved?

Not established from any source found so far. The office funding the initiative and formally participating in its governance is a structural fact; whether it was raised internally as a concern before the April 2025 grant vote is unknown.

Is "World Trade Center Utah" the same organization as "Utah World Trade Center Corporation"?

Not confirmed. This project has encountered similar naming variances with other entities before (e.g., LMSA-related naming questions elsewhere in this research); the two names here are similar but not identical, and no direct confirmation has been found either way.

What is Utah Rising's current status, following the grant's cancellation?

Resolved in part: the initiative is still active. Its own website lists recent news items including "Advocacy in Action: Utah Rising Priorities Take Center Stage in the 2026 Legislative Session" and "From Vision to Action: 2026 Legislative Session Outcomes" — meaning Utah Rising remained an active legislative advocacy presence through the same 2026 session that produced both H.B. 545 and the GOED audit, more than a year after its own IAA grant was canceled. Still not established: whether it received funding from any other source after the cancellation, or whether its Steering/Technical Committee membership has changed since April 2025.
