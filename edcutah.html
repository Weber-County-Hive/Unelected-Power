EDCUtah: who pays it, and what it markets

A private business group inside the Salt Lake Chamber recruits companies to Utah and certifies large sites for development. Cities, counties, a state authority and the state itself are among its investors. Several of the sites it markets sit where data centers, tax increment zones and state tax credits are already in the record.

What EDCUtah is

The Economic Development Corporation of Utah (EDCUtah) was founded in 1987. It describes itself as "a private, non-profit organization" and a "catalyst for quality job growth and increased capital investment in the state." It is registered with the IRS as a 501(c)(6) business league (EIN 74-2502619) and reported about $1.5 million in revenue for the fiscal year ending June 2024. It merged into the Salt Lake Chamber in July 2024; the Chamber's president, Derek Miller, is listed as its principal officer.

Its president and executive director since May 2025 is Ryan Starks, the former executive director of the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity (GO Utah). A separate entity, the EDCUtah Foundation, lists Starks as a director since Feb. 24, 2021.

Its ties to the state, on paper:

  • March 27, 2026: GO Utah and EDCUtah signed a memorandum of understanding. GO Utah "leads strategy and positioning"; EDCUtah handles corporate recruitment, research and marketing to site selectors. GO Utah's director, Jefferson Moss, and Starks are both quoted.
  • Jan. 30, 2026: a Request for Appropriation asked the Legislature for $250,000 in one-time money for 2027, passed through to EDCUtah as a "Private Not for Profit," "supported by $2.2 million of private investment from Utah businesses." Whether it was funded is not yet confirmed.
  • EDCUtah sits on the Technical Committee of Utah Rising, the Chamber initiative that received, then lost, a $3.2 million state grant Starks approved before leaving GO Utah.
What is a 501(c)(6)?

A tax-exempt business league or chamber of commerce. Unlike a 501(c)(3) charity, gifts to it are generally not tax-deductible as donations, and it may lobby. It files a public Form 990, but its donor names are not public.

What it markets: the data center profile

EDCUtah publishes about 14 industry profiles. One is "Data Centers in Utah: EDCUtah Industry Profile 23-24." It is a sales brochure, not a government plan. In its own words:

  • "Utah offers lower costs for labor, construction, land, permits, and other vital operation and development costs."
  • "Utah enjoys some of the cheapest electricity and natural gas rates in the nation." (It lists 5.64¢ per kWh in Utah vs. 6.35¢ nationally.)
  • "Utah has some of the lowest water utility rates in the nation, with a water price index score of 46 (USA = 100)."

Words to know

Certified site vs. Mega site

Site Ready Utah's two labels. Certified sites are 100 to 400 acres, inspected by EDCUtah and outside engineers. Mega sites are over 400 acres; Stage 2 adds more engineering review and extra marketing.

EDTIF / REDTIF

State tax credits paid after a company meets its jobs and investment targets: up to 30% of new state tax revenue (EDTIF), or up to 50% in rural areas (REDTIF).

CRA / tax increment

A city or county freezes an area's tax value and sends the new property tax from growth to the project for a set number of years instead of to schools, the county and other taxing entities.

Site selector

A consultant hired by a company to choose where to build. EDCUtah markets Utah sites to them.
