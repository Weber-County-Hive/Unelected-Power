What this is

Jefferson Moss was a Utah lawmaker for eight years. In 2023 he became House Majority Leader. In May 2025 he resigned from the House. Gov. Spencer Cox appointed him to run the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity, called GO Utah. He started June 2, 2025. That job did not need a vote.

He replaced Ryan Starks. He also took over two of Starks' other seats: a non-voting seat on the MIDA board and a seat on the Inland Port Authority's Incentives Subcommittee.

What the records show

A fund he started, and the institute he runs. The Nucleus Institute lists Moss as the founder of the Utah Innovation Fund, a state venture fund. A 2025 law, H.B. 530, put the Nucleus Institute in charge of that fund. Moss leads the institute. The same law exempted both from Utah's open-meetings law and GRAMA, the state public-records law.

What the law still asks for. H.B. 530 says the institute must publish a yearly public report by Oct. 1, naming every business that got money from the fund and how much. It also needs a yearly audit sent to the state auditor and treasurer by Sept. 1.

Votes on his own facility. Moss sits on the Point of the Mountain State Land Authority board. Its minutes record four votes involving Convergence Hall, a Nucleus Institute building, from Feb. to May 2026. On Feb. 11, 2026, the board voted yes, all together, to make Convergence Hall's lot bigger. The minutes list Moss there. No recusal (stepping aside from a vote) is recorded. On April 8, 2026, he was listed absent.

Campaign money to a fellow board member. Jordan Teuscher co-chairs that same board. Moss's campaign has given $6,750 to Teuscher or to a PAC Teuscher's campaign also funds. The last payment was $1,000 on Jan. 14, 2026, about four weeks before the Feb. 11 vote. Moss's campaign also gave $4,250 each to Rep. Candice Pierucci and to Anthony Loubet.

An audit. A Sept. 15, 2026 state audit of GO Utah, Report 2026-18, made six recommendations. Moss accepted all six.

What isn't known yet

Whether the Nucleus Institute has filed its Oct. 1 report and Sept. 1 audit. Its April 2026 report names the fund's 19 companies and says $4.9 million has gone out since 2023, but it does not list each company's amount.

State law says the institute's executive director must chair its board. The institute's own pages list Val Peterson as chair and Moss as a board member. Who was formally appointed is not confirmed.

Moss once worked as COO of the UVU Foundation while he was a lawmaker. When that job started and ended is not confirmed.

Why it matters to you

Moss holds several state seats at once. Some of those boards vote on things tied to his other jobs. The records let anyone see who was in the room and who paid whom.
