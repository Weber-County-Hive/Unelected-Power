Who Decides, Part 3: MIDA. Who writes its rules, and who checks it.

The Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) is a state authority run by an appointed board. Here is who sits on it, every bill that changed its powers from 2007 to 2026 with its sponsors and votes, how each change grades for the public, and the 2027 bills to watch.

The short version

  • MIDA was created in 2007 to develop land at and near Utah's military bases. Its board is appointed, not elected. In its project areas, county and city land use laws and ordinances don't apply (Utah Code 63H-1-202).
  • From 2007 to 2026, 22 bills that changed MIDA passed. Sen. Jerry Stevenson, who sits on MIDA's board, was the primary sponsor of 15 of them, in 15 of the 17 sessions from 2010 to 2026.
  • Some changes removed public steps, and they are still law. MIDA may set up its own board to hear records appeals instead of the state's records office (2021). MIDA doesn't have to physically post notice (2022). MIDA's board can add 15 more years to its tax share if it finds "significant benefit" (2019).
  • Some changes added public steps. Owners' valuation notices must show the payment to MIDA (2024). MIDA must now disclose before tax increment deals and bonds, and report every year (2026, S.B. 206).
  • Most MIDA bills passed by wide margins. The 2025 bill was the closest: 38-27 in the House.
  • For 2027, Sen. Brady Brammer and Rep. Casey Snider have filed MIDA bill requests ("Amendments" and "Limitations"). No text is posted yet.

Start here: what MIDA is

The Legislature created the Military Installation Development Authority in 2007 (S.B. 232). Its first job was Falcon Hill, a business park on Hill Air Force Base land.

Inside a MIDA "project area," MIDA acts as the local government for land use. It keeps up to 75% of the new property tax that comes from growth, for 25 years per parcel, and its board can add 15 more years. It can levy some taxes a city would normally levy, like an energy tax and an accommodations tax. Before MIDA can include land, the county or city must vote to consent, and the landowner must consent (63H-1-401).

The words used here, like "tax increment" and "project area," are explained in Words to know.

Bill-Tracker letter (C, D)
  • Transparency. 2021 and 2022 each removed a public step that is still law: MIDA may hear records appeals on its own appeals board instead of the state's records office, and MIDA doesn't have to physically post notice. 2024 added one (the payment shows on valuation notices) and extended the posting exemption to subsidiaries. 2026 S.B. 206 added disclosure and yearly reports.
  • Financial accountability. Whether to add the 15 extra years (2019) is decided by MIDA's own board. MIDA's PIDs (2020) can take on bond debt with the landowners' consent; like other PIDs in Utah, they don't need a public vote. MIDA's own 2023 presentation to the Legislature lists $71,194,600 in legislative appropriations for projects, FY2022–2024, while its 2026 slides say it "doesn't tap into State of Utah coffers" (see the MIDA hearing check).
  • Environmental impact. The 2023 contamination immunity, and the Stratos plan's water and power use, need a further look before a grade.
  • Local say. Since 2009 MIDA has been exempt from all county and city ordinances in its areas, not only land use. Since 2011, a county doesn't have to consent when all the land is inside a city. Since 2022, an owner's consent can't be taken back.

What went right (from the records)

  • Consent is still required. A county or city must vote yes and owners must agree before land goes in a project area (63H-1-401). Box Elder County voted to consent on May 4, 2026.
  • Audits and reports. A yearly report and an outside audit have been required since 2007 (63H-1-703 to 705). MIDA says its audits were clean. The State Auditor launched a public MIDA information page on May 28, 2026; the office says it "does not audit MIDA."
  • Elected members on taxes. A new tax or rate increase needs a majority of the board's elected members (63H-1-203).
  • New reporting. 2024 put the payment to MIDA on owners' valuation notices; 2026 S.B. 206 added disclosure and yearly reporting.
  • Military land. MIDA's own slides list 5 of its 8 project areas as mostly government or military land.

2026: Stratos and the reform push

  • April 24, 2026MIDA's board adopted the Stratos project area plan in Box Elder County, ten days before the county's consent vote (see the MIDA hearing check).
  • May 4, 2026Box Elder County Commission voted to consent.
  • Aug. 20, 2026Gov. Cox: "I think we need major reform when it comes to MIDA, and I will be pushing for that reform again" (Utah News Dispatch, Aug. 21).
  • Aug. 21 and Oct. 2, 2026Utah News Dispatch reported MIDA officials had paused Stratos; O'Leary Digital's CEO told KSL on Oct. 2 that "the project is not on hold." We found no MIDA board vote to pause.
  • Oct. 21, 2026MIDA answered a legislative committee's questions; the Hive checked 32 of its statements (MIDA at the Legislature).
  • Nov. 10, 2026House Speaker Mike Schultz told the Deseret News editorial board, "MIDA should never be doing data centers."
  • Nov. 18, 2026At the same committee, Rep. Paul Cutler presented the data center working group's 2027 goals: data center proposals, "including those from state economic development authorities (MIDA, UIPA, etc)," must go through "the same transparent process," and local governments keep the decision.

What the records don't show

Left out on purpose. An earlier Hive research note said the 2025 bill (S.B. 316) let MIDA control its own environmental reviews. The enrolled bill has no environmental review language, so it is not on this page. The same note said Stratos money could be spent at Hill Air Force Base. The 2025 law allows that kind of agreement only for project areas on state land or state armory board land, and we did not find a record showing Stratos is one, so that is not on this page either.
  • Which leader appointed which board member.
  • Whether MIDA has ever used the extra 15 years for a project area.
  • Whether MIDA's records appeals board has ever heard an appeal.
  • Whether the board ever voted to pause Stratos.
  • Whether MIDA received state money in FY2026 or FY2027.

Questions to ask, and who to ask

  • Senate president (from January 2027): Who will you appoint to MIDA's board? Will the seat go to a sitting legislator?
  • Your state legislators: Will the 2027 MIDA bills change the 2021 records appeals board, the 2022 posting exemption, or the board's power to add 15 years?
  • MIDA's records officer: How many records appeals has MIDA's appeals board heard, and how were they decided?
  • MIDA, or the State Auditor's MIDA page: Has MIDA used the extra 15 years for any project area?
  • Legislative Fiscal Analyst: What revenue could MIDA move outside a project area under the 2025 law, and for which areas?
  • MIDA board (minutes on utah.gov/pmn): Did the board vote to pause Stratos?
