UNELECTED POWER — CASE FILE

The Board Seat That Never Came Up

Heber City's top administrator sits on the board of the nonprofit his own office pays. Here's the paper trail — and the plain version, first.

The Weber County Hive

THE SHORT VERSION

  1. Heber City has a City Manager — the person who runs the city day to day. He isn't elected. His name is Matt Brower.
  2. Brower also sits on the board of a private nonprofit called CAMS, which runs Heber's downtown events.
  3. Brower's own office gave CAMS a city contract worth about $39,900 a year, for three years — nearly $120,000 total.
  4. CAMS's board — with Brower on it — voted to approve that deal on January 13, 2026. The City Council approved it a week later, on January 20, with Brower's staff report recommending it.
  5. When it came time to sign the final contract, there was no line for the Mayor. Only Brower signed for the City — even though the contract's own wording says the Mayor should.
  6. Someone filed a complaint with the Utah State Auditor. The City had to redo part of the paperwork to make the process look proper.
  7. Hundreds of Heber Valley residents signed a petition asking the City Council not to renew Brower's contract. The Council renewed it anyway, 4–2.
The man who runs the city gave nearly $120,000 in public money to a group he sits on the board of — and no one outside that arrangement signed off on it.

Who is Matt Brower?

Matt Brower has been Heber City Manager since 2018, appointed — not elected — by the City Council. City Managers in Utah's council-manager form of government run daily operations: staff, budget, contracts, and what makes it onto the Council's own agenda.

Before Heber, Brower held city manager posts in Lincoln, California (2015–18), Ocala, Florida (2008–14), and Santa Clara, Utah (2000–08). He describes himself as having "23 years" of city management experience.

Also on the record: Brower is a director of the Red White & Blue Festival Inc. (since 2023) and has sat on CAMS's board since 2019 — years before the city contract in question.

What is CAMS?

The Community Alliance for Main Street (CAMS) is a Heber City nonprofit that programs downtown events — the Heber Market on Main, Halloween Feast, Old Fashioned Christmas, and more. Its own bylaws describe it, oddly, as "this advisory committee," even though it now operates and contracts as an independent 501(c)(3) corporation.

CAMS's board as of January 2026: Tom Stone (Chair), Matt Brower, Dallin Koecher, Jeff Bradshaw (Treasurer), Ryan Fritzsche, Kelsey Berg, Erik Rowland, and Nicole Ferguson. Its one paid employee is Executive Director Rachel Kahler. Tap a card.

The signature nobody expected

The final, signed Professional Services Agreement contains a standard recital: "City caused this Agreement to be signed by its mayor and attested by its city recorder."

The actual signature block that follows has no line for the Mayor at all. It has one line: Matt Brower, City Manager.

The same person who sits on CAMS's board, who helped approve this deal as a CAMS board member on January 13, and who authored the staff report recommending it to the City Council — is also the only person who signed for the City.

What CAMS's own rules say about this

CAMS's bylaws, signed 2023, list among the powers reserved to its full Board of Directors: "Approve any self-dealing transaction, except as provided pursuant to Law."

In other words, CAMS's own governing document anticipated exactly this kind of situation — a board member with a competing interest in a CAMS transaction — and built a required approval step for it. Whether the Heber City contract was ever formally treated and approved as a "self-dealing transaction" under that provision is not shown in any document obtained so far.

What the contract itself pays CAMS

$39,900 per year, paid quarterly, for a term running through 2028–29 with automatic annual renewal. CAMS also keeps most event-sponsorship revenue, splits Heber Market on Main sponsorships 50/50 with the City, and can pursue additional TAP tax grants on top of the contract. The City throws in police services, insurance, marketing support, and storage space in a former city administration building.

The money that doesn't add up yet

CAMS's 2024 financials show a single expense line — "Main Street Beautification" — of $347,465.54, roughly 90% of that year's entire budget. That figure collapsed to $4,000 in 2025. What the 2024 figure actually funded has not yet been identified in any document obtained. Separately, CAMS's "Program Support" line jumped from $600 in 2024 to $14,100 in 2025 — a 23x increase in a year total revenue fell 73%.

The public response

A petition titled "Petition of No Confidence," submitted April 14, 2026 by resident Bridget Whiting on behalf of "concerned residents of Heber Valley," collected 335 online signatures plus additional paper signatures — north of 300 total. It asked the Council to decline to renew Brower's contract, citing early renewal timing, salary increases, agreements with outside organizations, and declining public confidence. It explicitly did not allege criminal conduct.

Public comment emails submitted the same week raised additional, more specific concerns:

"Matt's recent statement in Park Record contradicts what was reported in Lincoln CA in regards to his knowledge of fraud." — Kelli Gomez, public comment, April 14, 2026
"I am aware that Sid Ostergaard has a conflict of interest with any decision made on the City Manager's contract renewal, since Sid personally benefited from a contract awarded by the City Manager a few years ago." — Tracy Taylor, Wasatch Taxpayers Association, April 14, 2026

Not everyone opposed renewal — at least one resident, Larry Saylor, wrote in support, calling Brower "honest and trustworthy and loyal." Opposition was organized and vocal, but not unanimous.

What's still unconfirmed

The Sid Ostergaard conflict-of-interest claim, the Park Record/Lincoln CA contradiction claim, what the $347,465.54 CAMS expense actually bought, and whether CAMS's board ever formally treated the City contract as a "self-dealing transaction" under its own bylaws — none of these are resolved by any document obtained so far. They are documented here as claims and open questions, not established facts.
