What this is

Steve Waldrip served two terms in the Utah House. In December 2023, Gov. Spencer Cox appointed him Senior Advisor for Housing Strategy and Innovation. On July 1, 2026, a new law, H.B. 68, made him state housing coordinator, in charge of the state's new Division of Housing and Community Development. He also chairs the Private Activity Bond Review Board. No voter picked him for any of these roles.

H.B. 68 says the housing coordinator needs the Senate's "advice and consent." Whether the Senate has voted on that is not known.

What the records show

The fight. A 2024 law, SB 258, lets landowners ask the state to form a new "preliminary municipality," a new town, without the county's OK. Wasatch and Summit counties oppose it. On Aug. 12, 2026, their leaders agreed to seek its repeal. Around Aug. 18, Wasatch County turned down a 144-unit proposal on state trust land above Heber.

The message. On Aug. 18, 2026, Waldrip spoke to the Heber City Council. He said, per the Park Record: "So my message back from our good governor, who cares deeply about these issues is: Your funding as a county is now at risk, meaning none. Anything within my power, I will stop." He named Class B and C road money as suspended.

What the road-money law says. B and C road money comes from gas taxes and fees and goes to every county by a set formula: half on road miles, half on population. Under the state rule, R926-3, UDOT, not the Governor's office, can hold back that money, and only for certain problems with how it is spent or reported. Opposing a law is not on that list. Neither Cox's office nor Waldrip named any such problem.

How the counties answered. Wasatch County Manager Dustin Grabau, asked if it was a threat, said: "It certainly came across that way." Summit County's finance officer said, through Councilor Canice Harte, that the county was not aware of any formal notice from the state suspending funding for local programs. The Governor's office did not answer the reporters who asked.

What happened next. After an Aug. 28, 2026 meeting, Wasatch County said there would be "no negative impacts to state funding" (Utah News Dispatch, Sept. 21, 2026). Wasatch County passed Resolution 26-11 on Sept. 2, 2026.

PIDs. At the same meeting, Waldrip defended Public Infrastructure Districts, or PIDs, as a way to pay for new roads and pipes, calling it "cost before or cost after." With a PID, new homeowners pay that cost back through an extra charge on their tax bill for years. In everything the Hive reviewed, no one has published side-by-side math showing a PID home costs a buyer less in the end.

What isn't known yet

Whether Cox told Waldrip to say those exact words, or whether Waldrip used his own words for the governor's general view. No outlet has Cox's own account. No withheld grant, loan or budget line for either county has been documented.

Why it matters to you

Road money is set by a formula in state law. When an appointed official says it is "at risk," it is worth knowing who has the power to hold it back, and why.
