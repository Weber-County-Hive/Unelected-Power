What this is

Dan Hemmert has held four jobs that all touch the same state business world.

1. State senator. He was appointed to the Senate in 2016 and represented Senate District 14 in Utah County until January 2021.

2. State office director. He left the Senate to run the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity (GOEO) for Gov. Spencer Cox, for about a year. Ryan Starks and Jefferson Moss later ran the same office.

3. Lobbyist. From 2023 to August 2026 he was paid to speak to lawmakers for clients, under the name Hemmco.

4. Head of MIDA. MIDA's board made him its Executive Director. His start was reported as Aug. 1, 2026. He told lawmakers in October that MIDA has 13 employees and 8 project areas, 3 of them active.

What the records show

His lobbying clients included Dakota Pacific Real Estate, Alterra Mountain Company, Union Pacific Railroad, Zions Bancorporation and MIDA itself.

The state lobbyist list shows most of his clients, including MIDA, ending on Aug. 3, 2026. That is two days after his reported MIDA start.

Two cities paid his firm. Tremonton City paid Hemmco $365,000 from 2023 through 2027 (budget years). Perry City set his pay at $8,000 a month.

Two people who each gave $5,600 to his 2020 Senate campaign are tied to his later clients. Victoria Miller is married to a founding partner of Dakota Pacific. Lance Bullen leads Colmena Group, and Colmena Capital is on his firm's client list.

Attorney General Derek Brown's own disclosure form lists him as a member of LH Capital, LLC. The state business record names Hemmert as that company's only listed leader.

A company tied to Hemmert's business entities, BizDevOps Consulting, got $62,500 in Oct.-Nov. 2024 from a taxpayer-funded housing nonprofit. That payment was left off the nonprofit's year-end report to the state.

On Oct. 21, 2026, a lawmaker, Rep. Dunnigan, told Hemmert that posting MIDA notices only on a website is "not adequate." Hemmert said a local notice rule "might be a great modification." He also said he knew of no "but-for" study for the Wasatch project area.

What isn't known yet

  • When Attorney General Brown became a member of LH Capital, before or after Hemmert's move to MIDA.
  • Whether MIDA has a conflict-of-interest process for matters involving his former clients, such as Dakota Pacific.
  • Whether "ALL IN FOR UTAH" (2023) and "All In For Utah PAC" (2026) are the same group.
  • A claim that his firm worked for both Eagle Mountain and Meta on the same tax fight comes from a secondary source. It has not been checked.

Why it matters to you

Hemmert has not been charged with anything. There is no state rule that sets a waiting period before a lobbyist can lead an authority he lobbied for. The records show each step, with dates, so you can see it for yourself.
