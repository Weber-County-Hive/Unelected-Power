What this is

On Aug. 25, 2026, an ICE agent detained a man in Ogden, near 22nd St. and Porter Ave. An Ogden police officer was there. The man is called "Jonathan" on the video.

Only two people made decisions in that moment: the officer and the ICE agent. Nobody voted for either one. The people Ogden does vote for, the mayor and the city council, were not there. They spent the next weeks answering questions about it.

What the records show

What OPD says. The police department says the ICE agent first asked the officer to pull the man over, the officer said no, and the agent drove off. That story comes from OPD itself, as told to KUTV. It is not confirmed anywhere else, and it is not in the body-camera video the Hive reviewed.

What the video shows. The officer walks over to the ICE agent's car on his own. He asks the agent if he has "a reason to stop them." He says he will stay "and make sure that [nothing goes south]... but I can't help." Then the agent detains the man.

Two different stories about Jonathan. DHS told reporters he is "an immigrant in the country illegally from Venezuela." On camera, Jonathan said he was here legally and had been working, delivering food. No court record settles it.

What the city said. On Aug. 26, OPD said it "did not initiate the stop, did not conduct the arrest, did not ask for or run any information, and did not assist with immigration enforcement." At a Sept. 1 council meeting, Councilwoman Alicia Washington asked for a full review. In September, the mayor and chief said a written ICE policy was "potentially" coming, with no timeline.

What state law says. A Utah law, §76-14-205, says a city may not adopt a rule that stops its police from working with federal immigration agents. It does not make any officer help. A 2026 bill, H.B. 571 by Rep. Trevor Lee, would have made police actively help, with civil fines up to $5,000 against individual officials. It never got a hearing and was filed as not passed on March 6, 2026.

A different choice nearby. Weber County's sheriff, who is elected, signed a 287(g) agreement with ICE. That is a choice he made, not one the state law required.

What isn't known yet

Whether the officer really said no before the video starts. Whether Jonathan is here legally. When, or if, Ogden will write an ICE policy. The full case file also points out that the city has not published a simple explainer of what the law already allows, and that nothing in the law appears to stop it.

Why it matters to you

The people we vote for are not always the people making the decisions that affect us. Here, the choices on the street were made by people no Ogden voter can reach.
