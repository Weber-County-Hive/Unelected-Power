PLAIN WORDS · POSTAGE PERMIT · 2026 · UP 100.14

The party's postage permit, in plain words.

The short version of the State Party Indicia case file.

Posted Nov. 5, 2026 · Updated Nov. 8, 2026

Correction, Nov. 8, 2026: An earlier version said Tactical Campaigns gave the Utah Republican Party $49,185 and was paid $118,501.77 by candidates and PACs. The 2026 filings show Tactical gave the party $71,600. Candidates and PACs paid Tactical $117,151.77; the party itself paid it another $1,350.

The stamp deal

Mailing letters costs money. The Post Office gives some groups a cheaper price. A state political party is one of them.

The Post Office has a rule about that cheap price. The party can use it only for the party's own mail. It can't let someone else use it, and it has to pay for the stamps itself.

What the party's rule book says

The Utah Republican Party has a rule book. One page says candidates can use the party's cheap price. Here is how it works:

1. The candidate
Pays a printer to print the mailers.
↓gives the party the stamp money, plus about 10% more
2. The party
Says yes, mail it with our cheap price.
↓the Post Office takes the stamp money from the party
3. The Post Office
Sends the mailers to voters.
An example. Say the stamps for a candidate's mailer cost $1,000. The candidate gives the party about $1,100. The party mails it at its cheap price.

Two rules side by side

The Post Office's rule

  • The cheap price is for the party's own mail.
  • The party pays for its stamps.
  • The candidate does not pay the party back for them.

The party's rule book

  • Candidates can use the cheap price.
  • The candidate gives the party the stamp money.
  • Plus about 10% more.

What the records show

Candidates have to report the money they spend. So does the party. In 2026:

  • Republican candidates and groups reported paying the party $441,267.67.
  • The party reported paying the Post Office $305,345.98.
  • Most of it happened right before the June primary and right before the November election.

A question: money through a helper company

The rule book also says the party works with certain printing and mailing companies that "know the process." Their names are blacked out.

Here is why that matters. If a candidate pays a helper company, and the helper company pays the party, then:

  • The candidate's report only shows the helper company.
  • The party's report only shows the helper company.
  • Nobody reading the reports can see the candidate used the party's cheap price.

One campaign helper company, Tactical Campaigns, gave the party $71,600 in 2026. Candidates and PACs paid it $117,151.77, and the party itself paid it $1,350 more. The records don't say whether any of that money was for stamps. That is a question, not a finding.

What we don't know

We can't tell from the records whether the extra 10% was charged, or which payments were for stamps. The Post Office decides whether a mailing followed its rules. No Post Office decision about 2026 was found.

Questions you can ask

  • The Lieutenant Governor's elections office: When a candidate uses the party's cheap stamp price, should that be reported as a gift from the party?
  • Your Republican State Central Committee members: When was this page of the rule book approved, and which mailing companies are on its list?
